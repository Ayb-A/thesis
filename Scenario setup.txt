### 4. The Three Exact Scenarios to Run

You will run all four models through these three exact timelines.

**Scenario 1: Constant Workload (The Control)**

* **Total Duration:** 2,000 timesteps.
* **Workload:** 50 tasks arrive every single timestep.
* **Objectives:** Weights are permanently locked at 0.5 for Latency and 0.5 for Energy.
* *Goal:* Prove the code runs without crashing.

**Scenario 2: Single Traffic Spike (The Adaptation Test)**

* **Total Duration:** 2,000 timesteps.
* **Steps 0 to 500 (Normal):** 50 tasks arrive per step. Weights are set to 0.3 Latency / 0.7 Energy.
* **Steps 501 to 1,000 (The Spike):** 150 tasks arrive per step. Weights dynamically shift to 0.8 Latency / 0.2 Energy.
* **Steps 1,001 to 2,000 (The Recovery):** Workload drops back to 50 tasks per step. Weights shift back to 0.3 Latency / 0.7 Energy.
* *Goal:* Prove dynamic weighting prevents latency bottlenecks during the 500-step spike.

**Scenario 3: Repeated Traffic Spikes (The Ultimate Stress Test)**

* **Total Duration:** 5,000 timesteps.
* **The Pattern:** 500 steps Normal $\rightarrow$ 500 steps Spike $\rightarrow$ 500 steps Normal $\rightarrow$ 500 steps Spike (repeating until step 5,000).
* **The Math:** Normal = 50 tasks (0.3/0.7 weights). Spike = 150 tasks (0.8/0.2 weights).
* *Goal:* Track the TD-error step-by-step. Prove that standard state-of-the-art algorithms (D3QN and SAC) suffer from policy collapse or slow recovery because constant shifting poisons their standard memory, while your MOPER artefact stays perfectly stable.
